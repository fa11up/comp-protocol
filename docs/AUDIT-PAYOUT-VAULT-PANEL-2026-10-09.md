## Resolution

| # | Severity | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | high | **Fixed.** The panel is right that the rate bounds the speed of a fall, not its size: `pace()` is permissionless and the keeper paces hourly, so a pool held at the attested low is paid 0.95 / 0.95^h of the pre-fall value after h paced hours, the whole 20% step after five (0.95^5 = 0.774), and a 5%-an-hour ramp inside the feeds' allowance was followed with no lag at all. The record's "about break-even" held for one hour and its "four paced hours" was five. The judge offered two fixes: a slower rate, or a second, slower reference. The rate is taken: `PAYOUT_PRICE_FALL_BPS_PER_HOUR` is 100 (1% an hour), so a 20% step reaches the payout in full after about 22 paced hours and a 40% step after about 51, and a profitable push has to be held on IMD's only market, against every dip-buyer, through the better part of a day rather than one feed window; the fee eats the first five hours of any hold. The slower reference was not taken: a 24-hour high is a ring buffer of storage for the same bound at a different shape, and the initcode has about 1 KB left. The constant's NatSpec now states what the rate buys and what it costs: a one-step fall reaches the payout over 1/rate paced hours, a held pool is paid the hours it is held, and after an honest fall redeemers are paid at the higher figure, falling 1% an hour, for that long (the direction that pays less, as before, now for a day). `cash`'s comment, the runbook and the risks page say the same. The panel's proofs are kept and pass: `test/payout-vault-panel/judge_high_HeldDownPoolRedemption.t.sol` (a two-hour and a five-hour hold at −20%), `test/payout-vault-panel/economics_high_RampedFallRedemption.t.sol` (five hourly 5% steps, paced on the hour); the intermediate assertion in each that the paid price had followed the fall in full is replaced by the bound the new rate gives (at most 5% in five paced hours). `test/RedemptionLagAtLaunch.t.sol` paces five days instead of one after its 70% fall. |
| 2 | low | **Accepted, with the bound stated** (at the constant, at `cash`, in the runbook and on the risks page), and its reach measured at the new rate: a pool pushed up 20% through one window and paced writes the rise at once; released, the paid price sits 20% above the attested one and decays 1% a paced hour, so every redemption is underpaid, by at most the push and never below the attested price, for about 18 hours. The panel's fix, writing the rise no faster than the fall, was not taken because it is the way back into finding 1: a rise written at 1% an hour leaves the paid price under the market after any real rally faster than that, and a pool pushed down 20% from the rallied level is then paid below the market by the gap (a 25% rally caught 5% by the paced figure gives 13% per imdUSD). The feeds admit 20% an hour, so closing that window means writing the rise at the feeds' own rate, which is at once. Nothing is taken in the accepted direction: nobody is forced to redeem, `minGemOut` lets a redeemer wait, and the push is paid for by whoever makes it. `test_aPumpThroughOneWindowUnderpaysRedeemersUntilItHasDecayed` in the kept judge proof measures it. |
| 3 | low | **Fixed.** `_tallyPrincipalRetired` nets out of a cancellation, after the transaction's own mint, the principal the position minted within `FRESH_DEBT_WINDOW` (its own record, `_recentlyMinted`, which redemption already keeps): the paced debt has had at most twelve hours to follow it, so cancelling it is not cancelling seasoned debt, and a draw one block old redeemed against itself the next block no longer clamps the paced debt by the whole amount. Netting out cancelled principal the paced debt had already followed is harmless: the clamp and the next pacing both hold the paced debt at or under the live debt, which the cancellation has lowered. A wipe is booked as before (everything but the transaction's own mint), since the wiped tally only restores the debt the transaction began with, which a repayment of young debt is part of. The residual is stated: a churner can draw, hold the debt twelve hours (fees, and 170% in IMD against it) and self-redeem to lower the paced debt by what the follow had not yet absorbed, which on a book near the 100,000 floor is most of a large draw; once a wage is set the aggregate ceiling is the accepted bound, and the wage is 0 at launch. The panel's proof is kept and passes: `test/payout-vault-panel/flow_low_CrossBlockSelfRedeem.t.sol`. |
